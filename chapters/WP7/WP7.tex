\section{Main advances in 2026}
\label{sec:wp7-main-advances-2026}

The June framework sessions identified applications for evaluating
HPDDM and PETSc, including the discrete fracture network mini-app and
a thermo-mechanical test case. Other sessions examined Kokkos porting
in TRUST, the PETSc interface in FreeFEM++ and adaptive meshes in
Samurai. The application specifications
and reported campaigns are collected in \Cref{chap:applications}.

The Ktirio Urban Building collaboration connects work across WP1,
WP6 and WP7. Terrain generation has a single-node scaling study. The
coupling adapter and statistical components support the wider
demonstrator, whose coupled performance has yet to be measured.

\subsection{PC5 working group on efficient high-order discretization}

The WP1/T1.4 contribution describes a wave-equation proxy application
and a portable high-order finite-element library developed within the PC5
working group on Efficient High-Order Discretization. The proxy provides
an application setting for CPU/GPU kernels through Kokkos, with high-order
continuous and discontinuous Galerkin formulations. Its methodological scope
and development steps remain in WP1; the related FUnTiDES wave-propagation
software is described separately in \Cref{sec:FUnTiDES:software}.

The wave application guides the kernel interfaces for CPU and GPU
execution. The Feel++ refactoring extends the discretizations
available for these studies with high-order finite-element families and
curved geometry (\cref{sec:Feelpp:implementation-2026:orders}). WP1 describes
the work on fused DG face terms and transfer operators for the proxy
(\cref{sec:wp1:t1.4}).

\subsection{PC5 working groups on block-structured AMR and unstructured meshing}

The Samurai fiche describes the Cartesian AMR methods available for
PC5 studies (\cref{sec:Samurai:software}). WP1 reports the corresponding
work on unstructured mesh adaptation and geometry processing

requirements remain to be recorded for D7.2.

\subsection{Ktirio Urban Building and the HiDALGO2 Centre of Excellence}

The Ktirio collaboration addresses three needs in city-scale energy
simulation: terrain and building geometry, coupling of microclimate and
building energy models, and execution and analysis of building ensembles.
\Cref{sec:pc5:ktirio-urban-building} shows how the components fit together.

The geometry workflow connects application inputs to Ktirio-Geom and its
benchmark application, \texttt{app-feelpp-ktirio-geom}
(\Cref{sec:app:specs:app-feelpp-ktirio-geom}). The Feel++ distributed ray
tracer computes solar masks. The Paris 8\,km campaign measured this phase
through 16\,384 MPI ranks on Discoverer
(\Cref{tab:Feelpp:solar-mask-discoverer}). The Building-only case also
completed at 32\,768 MPI ranks on 342 allocated Discoverer nodes with
simulation output disabled. Coupling urban microclimate and
building energy models motivates interface field transfer through preCICE
and the Feel++--OpenFOAM participants
(\Cref{sec:Feelpp:implementation-2026:precice}). For
\hyperref[sec:wp6:ensemble-streaming]{WP6/T6.4}, Ktirio implements ensemble
execution and in-run statistical analysis. Feel++ supplies the MPI
infrastructure and reusable streaming-statistics components
(\Cref{sec:Feelpp:implementation-2026:stats}).

The terrain campaign supplies timings for the geometry stage. The MPI
ensemble workflow and its reducers have dedicated tests. The geometry
timings do not quantify the cost of the ensemble workflow.
