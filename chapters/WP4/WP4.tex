\section{Objectives \& Context}

\label{sec:wp4:objectives}

Work Package 4 focuses on combining data and models through the formulation and solution of inverse problems. 
The approach covers both deterministic and stochastic models and methods, considering observation sparsity and sufficiency. 
Multi-fidelity models (MFM), including reduced-order and AI-based surrogates, are studied for the solution of inverse problems at exascale.

\paragraph{Relation to Exa-MA Bottlenecks.}
WP4 directly addresses several exascale bottlenecks:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{[B7] Exascale Algorithms:} Redesigning inverse problem algorithms to improve scalability, reduce communication, and enhance computational efficiency.
\item \textbf{[B8] Discovery, design, and decision algorithms:} Focus on ensembles of many small runs for uncertainty quantification and parameter optimization in inverse problems.
\item \textbf{[B9] Resilience, robustness and accuracy:} Ensuring inverse problem solutions are correct, reproducible, and verifiable.
\item \textbf{[B10] Scientific productivity:} Providing tools for scientists to use exascale systems productively for data assimilation and inverse problems.
\item \textbf{[B13] Opportunity to integrate uncertainties:} Directly incorporating uncertainties into the core of inverse problem calculations.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Strategic Role in Exa-MA.}
WP4 builds upon the foundational work of WP1 (discretization), WP2 (model order reduction and surrogates), and WP3 (solvers) to enable:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Deterministic inversion:} Variational methods with time-parallel approaches and hybrid ensemble-variational techniques.
\item \textbf{Stochastic inversion:} Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) for realistic system representations.
\item \textbf{Multi-fidelity strategies:} Leveraging surrogate models (from WP2) to accelerate inverse problem solutions through optimal scheduling between high-fidelity and reduced-order models.
\item \textbf{Observation strategies:} Improved treatment of observation error statistics and alternative metrics based on transport theory.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Key Tasks (T4.1--T4.5).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T4.1 Deterministic methods:} Enhance variational methods with time-parallel approaches and hybrid ensemble-variational techniques.
\item \textbf{T4.2 Stochastic methods:} Develop stochastic inversion based on Itô theory (SDEs) and SPDE formulations.
\item \textbf{T4.3 Observations:} Improve observation error statistics modeling and develop alternative metrics based on transport theory.
\item \textbf{T4.4 Multi-fidelity modeling:} Leverage reduced-order models and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) from WP2.
\item \textbf{T4.5 Multi-fidelity scheduling:} Develop optimal scheduling strategies for combining high-fidelity and reduced-order models.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Starting Activities (October 2025).}
WP4 activities spun up in October 2025, with two PhD students beginning their research:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PhD 1:} On Bayesian Inverse Problems for Wave Propagation towards Exascale, on the topic of Ensemble and Machine Learning Methods for Bayesian Inverse Problems. 
\item \textbf{PhD 2:} Computational Bayesian optimal sensor placement for ocean models.
\end{itemize}

The work will be conducted in close collaboration with Inria teams (lead), UNISTRA, and external partners including CEA and industrial stakeholders interested in data assimilation for climate modeling, geophysics, and energy applications.

\subsection{Task 4.1 - Deterministic methods}

Operational four-dimensional variational data assimilation (4D-Var) systems are moving toward unprecedented resolutions and model complexity, requiring scalable algorithmic solutions suitable for next-generation exascale supercomputers. The dominant computational bottleneck in incremental 4D-Var lies in the inner loop, which repeatedly solves high-dimensional linear systems using conjugate gradient iterations. While spectral Limited-Memory Preconditioners (LMPs) can significantly accelerate convergence, building them via exact or Krylov-based eigensolvers becomes computationally prohibitive and essentially serial. To address this, the current project task focused on designing and benchmarking highly parallelizable preconditioning techniques using multi-fidelity modelling and randomised linear algebra.

The methodology relies on a randomised sketching approach based on the Nyström approximation to construct spectral LMPs during the critical first outer-loop iteration. Because matrix-vector products within this sketching phase are completely decoupled, the design achieves excellent parallel scalability across large compute node allocations. Additionaly rather than applying costly high-fidelity model propagators during the preconditioner setup,we evaluated the use of lower-fidelity representations, such as reduced-precision operators or simplified physical propagators. Using Löwner ordering and perturbation theory, explicit bounds were established to characterize when a low-fidelity constructed preconditioner guarantees improved condition numbers for the high-fidelity system.

For scenarios where this strict theoretical condition is not satisfied, such as over longer assimilation windows, an alternative hybrid multi-fidelity sketching strategy was developed. This approach captures the dominant eigenspace by enriching random subspaces with inexpensive low-fidelity applications before performing minimal high-fidelity refinement. These approaches were numerically validated on a two-dimensional Shallow-Water equations benchmark in a double-gyre oceanic circulation setup.  

\subsection{Task 4.3 - Observation}

Designing an optimal observation network for geophysical applications requires strategically placing sensors to capture the most valuable data while strictly limiting the number of costly physical deployments. In Bayesian optimal experimental design, this challenge is often formulated as the maximization of the Expected Information Gain (EIG) to select the most informative data acquisitions. This capability is critical for next-generation exascale systems where physical experiments or high-fidelity simulations are exceptionally expensive. However, computing the EIG for nonlinear forward models and non-Gaussian priors—which are typical in complex earth sciences—is usually prohibitive. Standard estimators rely on costly nested Monte Carlo sampling that scales poorly and creates severe computational bottlenecks. To overcome these limitations, the current project task focused on establishing a tractable, theoretically grounded framework for large-scale experimental design by deriving new certified upper and lower bounds on the EIG.

Using dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, this work reformulates the EIG bounds entirely in terms of signal-to-noise ratios, preserving a simple matrix-based structure that relies solely on covariance and Fisher information matrices. To that goal we introduce two distinct families of bounds: conservative bounds designed to retain the maximum information content of the entire dataset, and incremental bounds tailored for sequentially designing the observation network. To further reduce computational burdens, we introduced practical approximations for the Fisher-information matrices that require only a small number of forward model gradient evaluations, effectively bypassing the nested-sampling bottleneck altogether.

These approaches were numerically validated using a parametrized partial differential equation based on the viscous Burgers model, which is often used as a proxy for fluid and geophysical dynamics. The experiments demonstrated that the tightness of the proposed bounds is directly controlled by the nonlinearity of the forward model, and the method successfully recovers the exact EIG value in linear-Gaussian limits. By replacing expensive nested sampling with scalable, gradient-based matrix diagnostics, we provide an efficient, highly parallelizable alternative to direct EIG estimation.
