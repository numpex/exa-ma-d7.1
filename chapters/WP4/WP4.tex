\section{Objectives \& Context}
\label{sec:wp4:objectives}

WP4 focuses on combining observations with large-scale physical models
through the formulation and solution of inverse problems at exascale
(led by UNISTRA with major contributions from Inria, and in collaboration
with \exadost{} and \exaatow{}). The work covers both deterministic and
stochastic formulations, addresses observation sparsity, error models and
experimental design, and exploits multi-fidelity model hierarchies---including
reduced-order and AI-based surrogates---in coordination with discretizations
from WP1, reduced models from WP2 and solvers from WP3. In practice, WP4
comprises the following tasks:

\begin{itemize}
\item[T4.1] Deterministic methods. Variational methods are inherently
  sequential, so additional sources of parallelism are needed. Promising
  avenues include time-parallel weak-constraint formulations, exploiting
  distinct physical, spatial and temporal scales, parallelizable
  preconditioning strategies, and hybrid ensemble--variational approaches
  using ensembles to better represent error covariances.
\item[T4.2] Stochastic methods. Realistic representations of physical
  systems can be formulated through stochastic processes governed by
  stochastic differential equations (Itô SDEs) or stochastic partial
  differential equations (SPDEs). This task investigates and compares
  such stochastic formulations, alongside ensemble-based inversion, on
  inverse and data assimilation problems made tractable by exascale
  computing.
\item[T4.3] Observations. Beyond the classical assumption of uncorrelated
  Gaussian observation errors, this task studies more general observation
  error statistics, alternative discrepancy metrics (such as transport-based
  metrics for position-dominated forecast errors), and observation-network
  or experimental design strategies.
\item[T4.4] Taking advantage of multi-fidelity modeling. Conducted
  conjointly with WP2 (Task~2.5), this task leverages hierarchies of
  lower-fidelity models, reduced-order models and AI-based surrogates
  (such as PINNs) to accelerate the solution of inverse problems.
\item[T4.5] Challenges of multi-fidelity in inverse problems: criteria to
  update reduced models. Passing across levels in a multi-fidelity
  hierarchy requires model-management and scheduling criteria to combine
  high-fidelity and lower-fidelity models optimally---either to reduce
  computational cost or to maximize precision and minimize variance---while
  scaling to exascale architectures.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scientific Advances}

During the reporting period, we have progressed on the following tasks:

\begin{itemize}
\item[T4.1] \phantomsection\label{sec:wp4:double-gyre}%
  In incremental four-dimensional variational data assimilation (4D-Var),
  conjugate gradients solve the symmetric positive definite quadratic subproblems
  in the inner loop, where preconditioning by the background error covariance
  $B = L L^\top$ leaves a preconditioned Hessian $A = I + \sum_n L^\top (G_n^k)^\top R_n^{-1} G_n^k L$
  whose smallest eigenvalue is $1$ and whose condition number is $\lambda_{\max}(A)$. In
  Hénon and Vidard~\cite{henon_multifidelity_2026} we construct a second-level scaled
  spectral limited-memory preconditioner (LMP),
  $P_r = I + S_r(\sqrt{\theta}\,\Lambda_r^{-1/2} - I_r)S_r^\top$ with scaling factor
  $\theta = \lambda_r(A)$, using a randomized Nyström low-rank approximation of $A$
  (\Cref{tab:advance:WP4-A2026-1}). Unlike Krylov/Ritz-pair LMPs that only become
  available from the second outer iteration onwards, the randomized sketch $Y = A^q\Omega$
  with Gaussian test matrix $\Omega \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times \ell}$ ($\ell = r + p$)
  is assembled from $\ell$ independent matrix--vector products and is immediately
  available during the first outer iteration, where the largest cost-function reduction
  occurs. Using $q$ power iterations improves spectral clustering at cost
  $\mathcal{O}(q\ell C_A + qn\ell^2)$, motivating the multi-fidelity extensions studied
  under T4.4 and T4.5. Early version of the method was showcased at the
  \href{https://exa-ma.numpex.fr/news/2026/20260119-21-ag.html}{January General Assembly}
  and application on a two-dimensional shallow-water
  double-gyre problem presented at the Ocean Sciences Meeting in February
  (\href{https://hal.science/hal-05652339v1}{\texttt{hal-05652339v1}}).

  \paragraph{Variational parameter identification (Feel++ / HiFiMagnet).}
  The HiFiMagnet inverse-problem specification seeks material, contact and
  cooling parameters from voltage, temperature and optional magnetic
  observations at several imposed-current operating points. The formulation
  combines a weighted multi-experiment data misfit with regularization,
  subject to the thermo-electric state equations and parameter bounds.
  Adjoint derivatives support optimization of the reduced objective.
  The proposed verification uses Taylor-gradient tests, parameter
  recovery and observation misfit
  (\cref{sec:app:specs:app-feelpp-hifimagnet-inverse}).
  The shared variational optimization interface is recorded in
  \Cref{tab:advance:WP4-A2026-2} and implemented in WP7
  (\Cref{sec:Feelpp:implementation-2026:tao}).
  
\item[T4.2] \phantomsection\label{sec:wp4:stochastic methods}%

The aim of Bayesian inversion is to provide a full description of a posterior probability distribution of the sought for parameters. An ensemble Kalman filter approach can treat nonlinearities, and is easily parallelised. To begin, MCMC inversion has been performed for the acoustic wave equation in two-dimensional domains, with varying material density. The results appear in the M2 (masters) thesis of B. Latre.

\item[T4.3] \phantomsection\label{sec:wp4:sensor-placement}%
  In Doumbouya, Vidard and Zahm~\cite{doumbouya_certified_2026} we develop a
  tractable, certified framework for Bayesian optimal experimental design (BOED)
  that avoids the $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ nested Monte Carlo bottleneck of Expected
  Information Gain (EIG) estimation (\Cref{tab:advance:WP4-A2026-3}).
  Using dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and bounds on the Fisher
  information matrices ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}^{-1} \succeq \mathbb{E}[I_{Y|\theta}]$
  and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{signal}}^{-1} \succeq I_Y$), we derive upper
  and lower bounds on $\mathrm{EIG}(W_m)$ expressed purely as Signal-to-Noise
  Ratios (SNRs) built from four $p \times p$ diagnostic matrices
  ($\Sigma_Y$, $\Sigma_{Y|\theta}$, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{signal}}$, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}$).
  The formulation unifies the standard ($Y = G(\theta) + \varepsilon$),
  nuisance-parameter ($Y = G(\theta, \eta) + \varepsilon$), and goal-oriented
  or likelihood-free ($Y = G(\eta) + \varepsilon$, $\theta = H(\eta) + \xi$)
  settings. Two complementary design criteria emerge: an incremental bound
  (\texttt{i-SNR}) tailored to sequential selection at small observation budgets $m$,
  and a conservative bound (\texttt{c-SNR}) aimed at retaining global information
  content for larger $m$. Both yield proven quasi-optimality guarantees controlled
  by the generalized eigenvalues of $(\Sigma_Y, \Sigma_{\mathrm{signal}})$ and
  $(\Sigma_{Y|\theta}, \Sigma_{\mathrm{noise}})$, recovering the exact EIG in
  the linear-Gaussian limit.

  Three strategies are proposed to assemble the diagnostic matrices offline in
  $\mathcal{O}(N)$ model evaluations, completely decoupling forward PDE solves
  from the algebraic $m \times m$ determinant evaluations of greedy sensor search:
  (i) pick-and-freeze sample estimators ($2N$ evaluations of $G$) for $\Sigma_Y$
  and $\Sigma_{Y|\theta}$; (ii) approximation-based denoiser estimators
  (\texttt{affine+nn}, using an affine map plus a three-layer GELU MLP of width $2p$)
  requiring $N$ solves; and (iii) gradient-based estimators using model Jacobians
  ($N$ Jacobian evaluations) that guarantee the Fisher-information inequalities and
  thereby preserve mathematical certification. In numerical experiments on a
  one-dimensional viscous Burgers equation (\href{https://github.com/mohamed495/CBOED}{\texttt{CBOED}}),
  varying the nonlinearity $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ confirms that the bounds collapse
  to the exact EIG at $\lambda = 0$, that gradient-based bounds are tighter at weak
  nonlinearity ($\lambda = 0.05$), and that \texttt{affine+nn} offers tighter
  intervals at strong nonlinearity ($\lambda = 1$) at the cost of losing strict
  certification.

\item[T4.4] \phantomsection\label{sec:wp4:multifidelity}%
  To reduce the cost of the sequential power iterations $q$ in randomized
  Nyström preconditioning for incremental 4D-Var, we introduce two multi-fidelity
  preconditioners exploiting a cheaper low-fidelity Hessian $A_{\mathrm{LF}}$
  with relative application cost $\nu = C_{A_{\mathrm{LF}}}/C_A \in (0, 1)$
  (\Cref{tab:advance:WP4-A2026-1}):
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Low-fidelity LMP (LF-LMP):} computes the entire randomized
    Nyström eigendecomposition on $A_{\mathrm{LF}}$, reducing sketching complexity
    to $\mathcal{O}(q\ell\nu C_A + qn\ell^2)$.
  \item \emph{Multi-fidelity LMP (MF-LMP):} constructs a hybrid sketch
    $Y = A^{q_{\mathrm{HF}}} A_{\mathrm{LF}}^{q_{\mathrm{LF}}} \Omega$ that
    enriches the random subspace with $q_{\mathrm{LF}}$ applications of
    $A_{\mathrm{LF}}$ before applying $q_{\mathrm{HF}} = 1$ high-fidelity
    Hessian product, at complexity
    $\mathcal{O}((q_{\mathrm{HF}} + \nu q_{\mathrm{LF}})\ell C_A + (q_{\mathrm{HF}} + q_{\mathrm{LF}})n\ell^2)$.
  \end{itemize}
  Three low-fidelity models are evaluated on a two-dimensional shallow-water
  double-gyre assimilation problem ($40 \times 40$ and $120 \times 120$ grids):
  a single-precision model ($\nu_{\mathrm{SP}} = 0.75$), a $20 \times 20$
  coarse-grid multigrid model with Shapiro-filtered transfer operators and a
  positive-semidefinite correction ($\nu_{\mathrm{MG}} = 0.2$ on $40 \times 40$,
  $0.15$ on $120 \times 120$), and a stationary/3D-FGAT model setting
  $M_{\mathrm{LF}} = I$ ($\nu_{\mathrm{stat}} \approx 0.007$--$0.009$).
  While LF-LMP depends strongly on the spectral fidelity of $A_{\mathrm{LF}}$
  (reducing total cost for single precision with $q = 1, 2$ and multigrid with
  $q = 1$, but offering no convergence gain for stationary), MF-LMP with
  $q_{\mathrm{HF}} = 1$ improves both PCG iteration counts and overall
  computational cost for multigrid ($q_{\mathrm{LF}} = 2$) and even for the
  inexpensive 3D-FGAT model ($q_{\mathrm{LF}} = 3$).

\item[T4.5] To assess when a low-fidelity model is suitable within a
  multi-fidelity preconditioning hierarchy, we decompose the spectral-norm
  range-approximation error $\Vert A - Q_{\mathrm{MF}} Q_{\mathrm{MF}}^\top A\Vert_2$
  into the standard high-fidelity randomized range error plus a multi-fidelity
  sketching perturbation $\Vert(Q Q^\top - Q_{\mathrm{MF}} Q_{\mathrm{MF}}^\top) A\Vert_2$
  (\Cref{tab:advance:WP4-A2026-1}). Using canonical angles between the orthogonal
  projectors $Q Q^\top$ and $Q_{\mathrm{MF}} Q_{\mathrm{MF}}^\top$, the projector
  distance is bounded by
  $\sqrt{2}\big(\sum_{i=1}^r (1 - \sigma_i(Q_{\mathrm{MF}}^\top Q)^2)\big)^{1/2}$
  (and analogously relative to the exact target projector $Q_* Q_*^\top$).
  Because $Q_{\mathrm{MF}}^\top Q \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ is small, these
  bounds are inexpensive to evaluate, establish a clear hierarchy among single
  precision, multigrid and stationary/3D-FGAT models, and explain the observed
  PCG convergence trends.

\end{itemize}

\section{Application Showcase}
\label{sec:wp4:applications}

\paragraph{Two-dimensional shallow-water double-gyre 4D-Var.}
The \hyperref[sec:wp4:double-gyre]{double-gyre assimilation study}~\cite{henon_multifidelity_2026}
evaluates LF-LMP and hybrid MF-LMP preconditioning on the first outer loop of
incremental 4D-Var over a 15-day assimilation window with altimetry-like surface
elevation observations, comparing single-precision, multigrid and stationary
(3D-FGAT) low-fidelity models.

\paragraph{Viscous Burgers Bayesian sensor placement.}
The \hyperref[sec:wp4:sensor-placement]{sensor-placement study}~\cite{doumbouya_certified_2026}
applies the \texttt{i-SNR} and \texttt{c-SNR} criteria on a one-dimensional
viscous Burgers model under standard, nuisance-parameter and goal-oriented
formulations, assessing bound tightness and sensor layouts as model nonlinearity
varies.

\paragraph{HiFiMagnet parameter identification.}
The \hyperref[sec:app:specs:app-feelpp-hifimagnet-inverse]{HiFiMagnet
inverse-problem fiche} specifies which material, contact and cooling
parameters to identify from observations. The
\hyperref[sec:Feelpp:implementation-2026:tao]{Feel++ interface to PETSc TAO}
provides the optimization components. Derivative checks and parameter
recovery on the application are planned under \nolinkurl{BM-FEEL-HIFI-INV-01}.

\paragraph{Ensemble Kalman inversion.}
The \hyperref[sec:app:specs:app-eki]{EKI mini-app} proposes a simplified
wave-equation inverse problem in a geophysical setting. Its PETSc/GeoS
workflow specifies MPI ensemble execution, a manufactured-solution
test and a weak-scaling protocol under \nolinkurl{BM-EKI-01}.

\section{Preliminary Benchmarks \& Trends}
\label{sec:wp4:benchmarks}

\paragraph{Multi-fidelity 4D-Var preconditioning benchmarks.}
The \hyperref[sec:wp4:double-gyre]{double-gyre benchmark}~\cite{henon_multifidelity_2026}
tests $40 \times 40$ ($\ell = 40$, $r = 35$, $n = 4\,880$) and $120 \times 120$
($\ell = 100$, $r = 80$) grids with relative low-fidelity operator costs
$\nu_{\mathrm{SP}} = 0.75$, $\nu_{\mathrm{MG}} = 0.2$ ($0.15$ at $120 \times 120$),
and $\nu_{\mathrm{stat}} \approx 0.007$--$0.009$. Under $n_{\mathrm{CU}} = \ell$
parallel sketching units, MF-LMP ($q_{\mathrm{HF}} = 1$) reduces overall
computational cost (measured in units of $C_A$) for multigrid ($q_{\mathrm{LF}} = 2$)
and 3D-FGAT ($q_{\mathrm{LF}} = 3$) on both grids, demonstrating that cheap
low-fidelity models ineffective in standalone LF-LMP can still accelerate
high-fidelity convergence when combined with one HF sketching pass.

\paragraph{Certified BOED sensor-placement benchmarks.}
The \hyperref[sec:wp4:sensor-placement]{viscous Burgers benchmark}~\cite{doumbouya_certified_2026}
($N = 200$ interior grid points, $10^4$ samples, up to $m = 15$ greedily selected
sensors) quantifies bound tightness across $\lambda \in \{0, 0.05, 0.2, 0.75, 1\}$
for standard, nuisance and goal-oriented settings. Results confirm exact EIG
recovery at $\lambda = 0$, tighter \texttt{i-SNR} vs.\ \texttt{c-SNR} brackets
in the small- vs.\ large-$m$ regimes, and the trade-off between certified
gradient-based bounds (tighter at weak nonlinearity) and uncertified
\texttt{affine+nn} estimates (tighter at strong nonlinearity).

\paragraph{Proposed registry benchmarks.}
Two proposed protocols are recorded in the
\hyperref[tab:v3-benchmark-registry]{benchmark registry}: the
\hyperref[sec:app:specs:app-feelpp-hifimagnet-inverse]{HiFiMagnet
inversion benchmark} (\nolinkurl{BM-FEEL-HIFI-INV-01}), specifying
Taylor-gradient checks and parameter recovery, and the
\hyperref[sec:app:specs:app-eki]{ensemble Kalman inversion benchmark}
(\nolinkurl{BM-EKI-01}), specifying manufactured-solution verification
and ensemble weak scaling.
