This mini-app couples two independent codes through Cwipi, the parallel
coupling library of ONERA \cite{quemerais_cwipi_nodate}. Both codes are built on
samurai \cite{bellotti_multiresolution-based_2022} (finite volumes on Cartesian
meshes, see \Cref{sec:Samurai:software}) and PETSc \cite{balay_petsc_2024}. One
solves the heat equation in a solid, the other the flow in a cavity heated from
the side, and the two exchange data at their common interface. The mini-app is the
reference case on which we validate the high-order multistep coupling scheme
implemented in Cwipi (see \Cref{sec:wp1:t1.7:multistep}), before its use in an HPC context.
It will soon be released as open source.

\subsection{Description of the benchmark}

The solid occupies $[-1/5, 0]\times[0,1]$ and the fluid the cavity $[0,1]^2$.
They meet at $x = 0$ (\Cref{fig:specs:samurai:cwipi}, left). The solid
follows the dimensionless heat equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:specs:samurai:cwipi:solid}
  \partial_t \theta_s = \frac{D_s}{D_f}\,\Delta \theta_s,
\end{equation}
with $\theta_s = 1$ on the left wall and a zero heat flux on $y = 0$ and
$y = 1$. The fluid follows the dimensionless Boussinesq equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:specs:samurai:cwipi:fluid}
  \partial_t \mathbf{u} + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}
  - \mathrm{Pr}\,\Delta \mathbf{u} + \nabla p
  = \mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Ra}\,\theta_f\,\mathbf{e}_y,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0,
  \qquad \partial_t \theta_f + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta_f = \Delta\theta_f,
\end{equation}
with no-slip walls, $\theta_f = 0$ on the cold wall on the right and adiabatic
horizontal walls.

The coupling is of Dirichlet-Neumann type. The fluid receives the interface
temperature from the solid and sends back its normal temperature gradient,
which the solid imposes as a flux weighted by the conductivity ratio
$K_s/K_f$. The coupling is nonlinear: natural convection makes the response of
the fluid, and therefore the interface flux, nonlinear in the imposed
temperature. The parameters are $\mathrm{Ra} = 10^4$, $\mathrm{Pr} = 0.71$,
$K_s/K_f = 1600$ and $D_s/D_f = 0.4$. The steady state
(\Cref{fig:specs:samurai:cwipi}, right) shows the convection cell in the
cavity. The solid is nearly isothermal because of the high conductivity
ratio.

\paragraph{Numerical method.}
Both domains are discretized with second-order finite volumes on samurai
Cartesian meshes. The solid solves the heat equation. The fluid solves the
velocity-pressure-temperature system as a whole, with exact incompressibility
and a Rusanov-type convection flux. In time, each code uses its own integrator,
here an 8-stage, fifth-order ESDIRK scheme from PETSc TS, and calls the
polynomial prediction of Cwipi at each stage.

\paragraph{Temporal convergence.}
The coupling time step $\Delta t$ is fixed. We measure the error on the coupling
variables at $t_f = 0.04$, in the middle of the transient, against a reference
solution computed with $\Delta t = 2.5\cdot 10^{-5}$. Repeating the run for a
sequence of $\Delta t$ and for several polynomial degrees $k$ gives the order
of convergence of the multistep scheme, which should be $k+1$.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
The next test case will be in 3D. Each code will adapt its mesh in space
independently of the other, so the two meshes will not match at the interface
and will change during the simulation. On this case, we will measure the HPC
performance of the coupling: the parallel behavior of each code and the cost of
the exchanges through Cwipi.

\subsection{Benchmarking methodology}
\begin{description}
  \item[Method-Algorithm WP1:] multiphysics coupling, finite volume
  \item[Method-Algorithm WP3:] multiphysics coupling, krylov solver
  \item[Framework:] PETSc, samurai, Cwipi
  \item[Parallel framework:] Sequential
  \item[Benchmark scope:] \texttt{method-verification}
\end{description}

The benchmark is a convergence study in time. The metric is the error on the
coupling variables at $t_f$ as a function of $\Delta t$, and the observed order
of convergence for each degree $k$ of the coupling polynomial.

\subsection{Input/Output Dataset Description}

\subsubsection{Input Data:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item No external dataset: the geometry, the boundary conditions and the
    meshes of the two domains are defined by the mini-app.
  \item Parameters: $\mathrm{Ra}$, $\mathrm{Pr}$, $K_s/K_f$, $D_s/D_f$, the
    coupling time step $\Delta t$, the final time $t_f$ and the degree $k$ of
    the coupling polynomial.
  \end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Output Data:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The coupling variables at the interface, from which the error against
    the reference solution is computed.
  \item The temperature and velocity fields of the solid and of the fluid.
  \end{itemize}

\subsection{Results summary}

The order $k+1$ predicted by the theory is observed up to order 4, on a
nonlinear coupling between two independent codes. The mini-app is therefore a
reference case to validate the high-order multistep coupling in Cwipi before
its use in an HPC context.
