\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

\begingroup
\small
\setlength{\parskip}{0.4em}
\begin{multicols}{2}

Exa-MA develops numerical methods and open-source software for
simulation and inference on heterogeneous exascale supercomputers, as
one of the targeted projects of the PEPR NumPEx programme funded
through France 2030. The consortium of five beneficiary institutions
and, with CNRS, ten associated laboratories has been extended into
2027. This mega-deliverable collects the contractual reports of the
project in a single document: the management and activity report
D0.2, the scientific activity reports and software deliverables D1.1
to D6.2, the benchmarking analyses D1.3 to D6.3 and D7.1, the
training report D7.3 and the WP7 coordination report D7.4. It is the
final v3.0.0 release of the 2026 collection.

The six scientific work packages advanced on complementary sources of
computational cost. WP1 delivered robust geometry processing and
constrained urban meshing in CGAL and Ktirio-Geom, high-order and
mixed finite-element discretisations in Feel++ 0.112.0, and a
distributed ray tracer for city-scale solar masks. WP2 rebuilt Scimba
in JAX around a differentiable discretisation layer (version 1.4.0)
and established sensing-dimension results for nonlinear reduced bases
with the NLCRBM++ implementation. WP3 extended domain-decomposition
preconditioners with stability guarantees, developed mixed and
adaptive precision Krylov methods, and released a PETSc-native GPU
backend for PCHPDDM. WP4 derived certified bounds on the expected
information gain for Bayesian sensor placement and a randomized
Nyström preconditioner for incremental 4D-Var. WP5 consolidated the
fractal decomposition family in Exa-Zellij: its fractal Bayesian
optimisation ranked first on 103 of 108 matched objective-budget
tasks, and its executions are now fault tolerant. WP6 combined MPI
ensemble execution with streaming risk analysis in Ktirio Urban
Building and released Uranie v4.9.0 with new sensitivity and
optimisation methods.

The 2026 campaigns quantify the performance of the methods at
exascale. Ktirio Urban Building, the Level~1 demonstrator, simulated
\textbf{207,624 buildings of the Paris 8\,km case on 32\,768 MPI
ranks} on Discoverer, tracing 471 million solar rays in 14\,min~55\,s;
doubling the ranks from 8\,192 to 16\,384 reduced the building-only
mask phase by 38\%. The Composyx domain-decomposition campaign
reached \textbf{4\,096 GPUs on JUPITER and LUMI} and solved a system
with more than one billion unknowns in 90\,s on LUMI, with
weak-scaling efficiency of 88 to 89\% on GPU partitions. Exa-Zellij
maintained 85\% strong-scaling efficiency for surrogate-based
optimisation and 61\% for a variational quantum eigensolver on
8\,000 LUMI-G GPUs. The Feel++ I/O study measured the cost of
preparing, writing and reloading simulation outputs, and a ReproHPC
audit verified the identity and integrity of the archived Ktirio
outputs. \textbf{The four milestones due on 14 October were achieved}:
software stack releases (MS2), benchmarking at exascale (MS3),
Level~1 demonstrator releases (MS4) and mini-app releases (MS5)
(\cref{tab:exama-milestones-october-2026}).

By 26 November, HAL recorded \textbf{47 research outputs for 2026}
and \textbf{77 since the project began}. The project counts
\textbf{18 published international peer-reviewed papers in 2026 and
37 cumulatively}, against a project target of more than 50
(\cref{tab:exama-publications-2026}); journal output in 2026 alone
exceeds that of the first three project years combined. Eleven of the
thirteen documented software families have a confirmed public release
in 2026. Exa-MA funds 31 appointments, of which 23 are in post
(\cref{tab:exama-staffing-2026}), and the 2026 training programme
continued with the MAJSC workshop on automatic differentiation in JAX
and four software exchange sessions with the HPDDM/PETSc, TRUST,
FreeFEM++ and Samurai teams (\cref{chap:training}).

The edition also records what remains open. Twenty-one of the
thirty-two documented applications and sixteen of the twenty-five
registered benchmarks have results still to report
(\cref{tab:exama-software-applications-benchmarks-2026}), and the
maturity of the WP4 and WP6 test cases flagged at the June review is
progressing. WP7 and the
scientific teams will document the conditions needed to reproduce
each application benchmark.

\end{multicols}
