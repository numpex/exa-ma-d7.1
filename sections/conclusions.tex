\label{sec:conclusions}

The 2026 edition of the mega-deliverable assembles the twenty
contractual reports due this period into a single document: the D0.2
management and activity report, the scientific and software
deliverables D1.1 to D6.2, the benchmarking analyses D1.3 to D6.3 and
D7.1, the training report D7.3 and the WP7 coordination report D7.4
(\cref{tab:deliverable-correspondence}). In its fourth year, extended
into 2027, Exa-MA has moved from method development to measured
exascale results: released software, reproducible campaigns and
demonstrators that connect the work packages end to end.

The scientific work of the period addresses each multiplier of
computational cost identified in the project. Discretisation and
adaptivity advanced from robust geometry processing and constrained
urban meshing to high-order and mixed formulations, with 22
registered verification cases for the mixed solid-shell
discretisation and a distributed ray tracer for city-scale solar
masks (WP1). Model reduction gained a differentiable JAX
discretisation layer in Scimba 1.4.0 and sensing-dimension results
for nonlinear reduced bases (WP2). Solvers progressed on three
fronts: domain-decomposition preconditioners with stability
guarantees for approximate subsolves, mixed and adaptive precision
Krylov methods, and GPU-native implementations of PCHPDDM in
PETSc~3.25 (WP3). Inverse problems gained certified bounds on the
expected information gain for sensor placement and a randomized
Nyström preconditioner for incremental 4D-Var (WP4). Optimisation
consolidated the fractal decomposition family, with fractal Bayesian
optimisation ranking first on 103 of 108 matched tasks and
fault-tolerant executions that survive changes in the MPI worker set
(WP5). Uncertainty quantification combined MPI ensemble execution
with streaming tail-risk analysis, verified by independent
reconstruction, and extended Uranie with new sensitivity indices and
optimisation methods (WP6)
(\cref{chap:wp1,chap:wp2,chap:wp3,chap:wp4,chap:wp5,chap:wp6}).
The Ktirio Urban Building demonstrator shows this chain end to end:
urban meshing from WP1, Feel++ solvers and coupling from WP1 and
WP3, and ensemble execution with streaming statistics from WP6,
coordinated by WP7 (\cref{sec:pc5:ktirio-urban-building}).

The 2026 campaigns quantify the distinct costs of large simulations
and provide reference runs for measuring how the methods behave
within larger workflows
(\cref{tab:exadi-ktirio-results,tab:Feelpp:solar-mask-discoverer,sec:app:specs:app-feelpp-io,sec:app:specs:app-composyx-diffusion,sec:wp5:benchmarks}).
On Discoverer, Ktirio Urban Building simulated 207,624 Paris
buildings at 32\,768 MPI ranks, tracing 471 million solar rays in
14\,min~55\,s; doubling the ranks from 8\,192 to 16\,384 reduced the
building-only solar-mask phase by 38\%. The Composyx campaign
reached 4\,096 GPUs on JUPITER and LUMI and solved a system of more
than one billion unknowns in 90\,s with 88 to 89\% weak-scaling
efficiency. Exa-Zellij maintained 85\% strong-scaling efficiency for
surrogate-based optimisation and 61\% for a variational quantum
eigensolver on 8\,000 LUMI-G GPUs. The Feel++ I/O study measured
preparation, writing and reloading separately, and the ReproHPC audit
verified the identity and integrity of the archived Ktirio outputs.
These campaigns, together with the Composyx runs on JUPITER, met the
October benchmarking milestone (MS3); all four milestones due on
14~October were achieved (\cref{tab:exama-milestones-october-2026}).

Software production and verification are the second achievement of
the period. Eleven of the thirteen documented software families have
a confirmed public release in 2026, including Feel++ 0.112.0,
Scimba 1.4.0, Composyx 1.7.1, HPDDM 2.4.0, PETSc 3.25, FUnTiDES 0.2.0
and Uranie v4.9.0 (\cref{chap:software}). Verification is anchored in
external references where they exist: the thermal-bridge case against
ISO~10211 (\cref{sec:app:specs:app-feelpp-discr-1}), building-energy
results against the ANSI/ASHRAE~140 BESTEST acceptance criteria, and
streaming statistics against independent reconstruction. Co-design
with the NumPEx PC5 working groups on high-order discretisation,
block-structured AMR and unstructured meshing, and with the Exa-DI
demonstrators Ktirio Urban Building, FUnTiDES and Samurai, records
the initial feedback that will structure the D7.2 co-design reports
at M48 and M60
(\cref{sec:wp7:codesign-initial-feedback,chap:exadi-demonstrators}).

The project's research record and community continue to grow. HAL
recorded 47 research outputs in 2026 and 77 since the project began,
including 18 published international peer-reviewed papers this year
and 37 cumulatively against a target of more than 50; journal output
in 2026 exceeds that of the first three project years combined
(\cref{tab:exama-publications-2026}). Thirty-one appointments are
wholly or partly funded by the project, 23 currently in post,
including six that started in 2026
(\cref{tab:exama-staffing-2026}). The training programme reached the
community through the MAJSC workshop on automatic differentiation
and four software exchange sessions (\cref{chap:training}). The
partnerships reported in D0.2 extend the reach of these methods:
JNL-G applies the multiphysics and model-reduction work to the
digital twin of the LNCMI high-field magnet facility, HiDALGO2
carries the urban-energy workflow into a European centre of
excellence, and MAXINET, INCORWAVE, BUTTERFLY, ENACT and the
industrial doctorates bring solver, coupling and inverse-problem
methods to test cases defined with their partners
(\cref{sec:management-year-4}).

The outlook for 2027 follows the priorities set at the June review:
verification and validation, GPU readiness, reproducible software
releases and integration across NumPEx.
Twenty-two of the thirty-three documented applications and sixteen of
the twenty-five registered benchmarks have results still to report
(\cref{tab:exama-software-applications-benchmarks-2026}); documenting
them, and the conditions needed to reproduce every reported run, is
the main editorial objective for the 2027 collection.
